# Commercial licence

Vaara is dual-licensed. You can take it under the AGPL-3.0-or-later, or under
a commercial licence. Both are live options and neither is a fallback for the
other.

## When you need the commercial one

The AGPL asks for one thing: if you run a modified Vaara and let others reach
it over a network, the users of that service can ask you for the corresponding
source of your modifications.

A commercial licence removes that obligation for your use. The usual reasons to
want it:

- embedding Vaara inside a product you ship closed
- running a modified version as a hosted service without publishing the changes
- distributing it to customers under your own terms
- an internal policy that will not accept copyleft in the dependency tree

## When you do not

If the AGPL obligation is fine for you, use Vaara and owe nothing. No
registration, no notice, no conversation. Compliance costs nothing and is not
a lesser tier.

Evaluating, testing, running it unmodified, and running it modified for your
own internal use without offering it over a network to others all sit inside
the AGPL.

## How it works

Henri Sirkkavaara is the sole copyright holder and grants commercial terms
directly. No committee, no foundation, nobody else to satisfy.

Write to hello@vaara.io with what you are building and how you intend to
deploy it. Terms depend on the shape of the deployment, so the conversation starts
there. There is no price list.

Asking is not an admission of anything, and nobody gets chased for asking.

## What a commercial licence does not do

It does not withdraw the AGPL version, which stays published and stays
available to everyone else. It does not buy a different codebase; it is the
same code under different terms. It does not include support, a service level,
or a pilot, which are separate arrangements.
